## Powers

> [!info|scimitar sheath]
> If the powers are a human body, then love is the body itself and the reality in which it lives.
### Power of Instinct 
The is a celebration of the subjective self with a focus on survival. Survival instinct is meant to protect our safety from all manners of harm. The self, whether the body or mind, is the only thing one knows to be real.  

> [!info|scimitar sheath]
> We don't need to learn how to use the power of instinct, but we can learn to use it better.
#### Self-aware
Being self-aware is simple, but it is complex. It's not only recognizing that one is alive. It also includes understanding who the self is from the closest and farthest perspective, how it prefers to operate, and what it wants to do. It can take a lifetime to understand the self to this degree.

**Qualities of being self-aware:** ^self-aware

1. **Self-identity:** This quality recognizes the Learner's individuality. There may be many similar people, but everything about the Learner is special: their thoughts, their beliefs, their purposes in life, and more. They cannot be replaced entirely. You matter.
2. **Perspective:** It's necessary to know that not only is the self an individual but that reality also consists of many other individuals living together. The Learner is but one of these with their own view. While that view is special, it's also quite limited. You matter, but you are not alone on the ocean.
3. **Healthy:** A person mustn't neglect their body. Despite a few health problems, a Learner is physically active and avoids unhealthy activities, sleep behaviors, and diets. One doesn't have to maintain a strict exercise routine and look like a muscleman, but it is important to have the discipline to maintain a body that is ready not only to move when needed, but think sharply.
4. **Self-love:** Being true to oneself is the ultimate sign of love for the self. Negativity has its place, but a person should be able to treat themselves with gentle fairness and painful truths with dignity. Self-love is also about appreciating one's own value, whether it's to the world or to themselves. The self has many things to give and teach the world.
5. **Survival Needs:** A Learner understands what they need to continue living in the world and that these are very few: food, water, air, shelter, sleep, exercise, safety, and solitude. Without a general sense of safety, it is very difficult for a person to work on personal issues and improvement because they are in danger. Further, being alone is essential to mental health. Although one doesn't need education nor socialization to survive, they certainly help. No matter the situation, the Learner does not confuse these needs with wants and desires.
6. **Human Nature:** The spirit of the human wants to be expressed in whatever way it chooses. In addition to survival needs, the Learner needs to be human in both nature and spirit. Human nature is to know what one is and what one is not, what one must to do to experience life in their chosen ways. The human spirit is born to be free, and it will seek freedom out when it's barred, whether that is in the form of external or personal oppression. 
7. **Self-respect:** A Learner knows the boundaries they must set for themselves to live in dignity. They know what they prefer and what they abhor. They know what keeps their dignity intact and what soils it. A person who possesses self-respect knows how to behave toward themselves, whether it is being honest or maintaining personal values despite how difficult or painful.  Respect begins here in everyone. Integrity is doing the right thing when no one is looking; honor is doing the right thing in public.
8. **Limitation:** A person in survival mode certainly knows what they can and can't do. Sometimes, ability can be the thin line between life or death. A Learner knows their limitations but does not begrudge them. They are a necessary tool used to derive meaning from experience. While many limitations are imposed upon everything by the rules of reality, many people unknowingly impose false limitations upon themselves via learned helplessness or conditioning. It can be fun to try to remove some where we can.
9. **Choice Control:** We must release the idea of being in control. A person controls nothing in reality. It detracts from the truth to think otherwise. The story is different when we think of our personal reality, what goes on in our own minds. We can control the choices we make. Our choices are met with reality's consequences, which we can't avoid. However, if we apply careful consideration to our choices, we can manage the risks well. Regardless of one's best efforts, though, the Learner understands the consequences are determined by nature. With practice, we can choose the emotions we feel when reality doesn't match the personal reality. Choosing how to react to those lessons of consequence can be one of the most interesting and rewarding puzzles. 
10. **Physical Resilience:** This is the humility that comes from being beaten down. A person having experienced physical conflict, whether it is fighting, addictions, being poor, living in survival mode, or else, knows a lot about being physically humble. This person understands what it's like to have nothing, to experience physical unfairness, fight to survive against threat, need help when injured, and/or to have been close to death. As a result, they gain a closer understanding of gratitude for what they have, who is around them, and who/what they want to protect. It's not just the idea of being safe, which is covered in the Survival Needs, but it's the knowledge, wisdom, and gratefulness for having had to crawl up from nothing. A Learner knows how helpful it is to appreciate the piece of bread in their stomach.

#### Self-sufficient
A self-sufficient person can depend on themselves for most of their needs and worries. They are both a source of comfort and motivation available at all times. A Learner isn't always strong because they are still human, but their traits help them endure life's intense pressures even through solitude. 

> [!info|piranha-header p5]- Qualities of being self-sufficient: ^self-sufficient
1. **Reality Separation:** A Learner knows the difference between their personal reality and the shared reality with everything else. What they believe to be real is secondary what is real. They know fantasy from fiction, belief from reality, and opinion from fact. They hold on to their inner reality, walk through the shared reality, and are resilient enough in both to endure damage from both. A Learner is capable of holding on to their inner truths without forcing others to believe in and participate with them.
2. **Unshakeable Unloneliness:** A person who is self-sufficient can be alone without anxious fear. It may be that they wish they weren’t alone, but they are not broken in isolation. Being alone is OK; being with others is OK. Solitude is needed in order to grow. In being alone, a person can find the quiet power within themselves. That is the same power that helps them be alone because it helps them resolve negative emotions, critically think to solve problems, assess risks, and make goals. 
3. **Courage to Try:** Though the world seems set to enjoy a person's failed efforts, it doesn't stop the Learner from being too afraid to try. It might not be the right time, the setting may not be perfect, and the team may not be ready, but launch anyway. A ship cannot see where the horizon leads by resting in the harbor with its sails furled, but it can rot away as it's spoiled by wasted time. This is the motivation that propels the self into the wild for the hope that something is actively found, not for something that is passively washed up on the shore. 
4. **Realistic Confidence:** A person who recognizes self-sufficiency understands the urgency of survival instinctively. A Learner no longer needs validation, affirmation, reassurance, praise, or even recognition for being themselves. Neither do they compare themselves to others and feel self-pity in the wake of it. They understand that everyone has their own background and personal problems, they are confident in carrying their own. They know the odds of survival and maintaining mental health are against them, but they move forward. This is the inner strength that keeps a person from giving up on life. If the courage to try is symbolized by the motivation behind a sailing ship, confidence is the keel of the boat keeping it from tipping over and sinking.
5. **Self-control:** Discipline serves a critical role in a person's life. It takes a great deal of mental strength to resist the temptations of instant gratification these days. Being told "no" by one's parents is the foundation to this trait. Without this, a person may not be able to exercise a filter against using violence to solve problems. It helps to be able to see delayed gratification as a higher reward. It's the idea of "work hard now, get the reward later" as opposed to "get the reward now, work later". It's the ability to compose oneself during intense emotional stress.
6. **Diligence:** A person can take the the small things for granted, mistakenly forgetting that the small things in life actually matter most. A Learner finishes a project not by doing the minimal effort but after applying their full effort, and doing so with integrity. Importantly, a Learner understands that failing is just what comes before success. It is a necessary part of improvement. The effort to survive and improve might not need to be a struggle, but can be (and most often is) in this reality. Failure is strength. How is this different than the courage to try? This is the monitoring effort of the trying. It's the difference between the captain getting on the ship at all (Courage to Try) and adjusting the rudders to steady the ship's sailing course.
7. **Pride, Not Hubris:** Many individuals deny themselves opportunities because of the false pride of arrogance, also known as hubris. People sometimes forget to realize that everyone has blind spots in which rest neglected issues. Pride in work that is actually done by oneself is not the issue, but the hubris of arrogance in believing that one is beyond things that may actually improve their lives. A Learner realizes that living in denial that one is beyond reproach or further education can be amazingly destructive for one's potential growth. This also symbolizes the ability to accept criticism and strong advice without becoming offended or defensive. Imagine a hull that is full of holes and the ship is taking on water. A captain proud of his responsibilities admits having allowed the hull to be damaged and starts to bail the water out with a bucket himself. A captain with hubris, however, refuses to acknowledge the mistakes that led to the damaged hull. He does nothing himself to start bailing the water to save his ship and crew. When someone is recognized for their helpful expertise, the feeling of being knowledgeable can balloons into related subjects, giving a false pride, wisdom, or knowledge where it doesn’t exist. The same could happen when someone defers to oneself. The Learner sees these and guards against them.

### Power of Emotion
This power consists of the reactions to reality and the consequences it gives us. When our inner reality doesn't match the shared reality, our expectations are damaged because we didn't get what we wanted. Reality is a neutral teacher: it does not favor us, nor does it despise us. However, it retains nearly full control of what happens to us. 

Emotions are our method to cope with what we can't control in reality. A Learner knows this, and instead of living in despair in a world full of limitations, they manipulate the only thing they can control: their choices.

Emotions are challenging, and we don't need to learn how to feel them. Instead, we need to learn how to understand them, how to appreciate the difficult lessons they teach, and how to let go of them when we accept their lessons. 

> [!info|scimitar sheath]
> Emotions want to be acknowledged, loved, and they want their freedom. When we ignore or hide them, we get sick mentally and physically. 
#### Emotionally-aware
This is when a person becomes aware of their emotions in the immediate moment they are being felt. When emotion rises, a Learner is first able to recognize their emotional state regardless of the situation or their personal connection to the subject matter. Realizing emotions are present is the first step toward reconciliation of both feelings and relationships.

What we feel to be true is different than what is true in reality. The separation is very clear to a Learner. Despite this, one must operate within the boundaries of one's self-awareness and -respect. The existence of one's self cannot be denied to exist, and self-expression should not be oppressed in any circumstance–literally *any* circumstance at all.

**Qualities of being emotionally-aware:** ^emotionally-aware
1. **The Pause:** This is when the person takes that breath which separates them from their emotions and the moment. It is the soulful  sidestep that shoots them into the Power of the Witness so that they may be aware of what is really happening.
2. **Emotional Intelligence:** This is the understanding of what emotions are, what they do and need, how they impact a person, and how to process them. It's the understanding that emotions are natural reactions to the mismatch between reality and one's personal reality. A Learner understands that emotions are teachers, not threats. Hiding or suppressing them is harmful mentally and physically, so emotions seek to be felt, understood, and expressed. Other people's emotions can also be understood vicariously through empathy. By putting oneself in other people's place, one changes the frame of reference. Their feelings become the observer's feelings. In the end, a Learner can say, "I understand," and they truly do. 
3. **Emotional Management:** This means that a person has the ability to manage their own emotions without assistance and without relying on others for validation or support. It's the knowledge that one's own emotions should never impact others because personal problems are managed by the self. Regardless of fault or blame, a Learner understands that dumping negative emotions on innocent people is never an option. It may be that a person with this quality may still seek guidance with a particularly troublesome emotion. This does not mean that a person should only keep emotions to themselves. Emotions are meant to be expressed, but do so wisely and with courtesy to others. 
4. **Integration:** This is the absorption and application of life lessons learned from processed emotions. It bridges insight from the lesson and living the lesson. When a person receives new information, the mind goes into a state of imbalance because reality doesn't match their personal reality. This is a confusing, uncomfortable feeling, and it may take a person time to accommodate the lesson. A Learner understands that integration is vital to growth because ignoring the truths of reality ends disastrously. In this moment, patience and resilience are vital helpers.
5. **Accountability:**  This is the sense of being responsible for one's actions, rightfully taking the blame, and expressing their apologies. This is the sense to do what is right by their own set of morals and the set of those they choose to be with. On the other side, they must also recognize where accountability that is not theirs should go. Things beyond one's control (there are a lot!) should not be accepted as one's own fault. The wisdom to separate those is important.
6. **Emotional Resilience** This is the person’s ability to bounce out of negative mental states, societal unfairness, or social drama. Downward spirals of emotional pain can turn into a dangerous whirlpool of recurring cycles, but the Learner defends themselves with the help of their support group and their personal power. Not only this, but a person with this ability boosts themselves into a positive mindset, like a sudden windfall that pushes the their ship away from the shoal.
7. **Genuine Authenticity:** A person confident in themselves is able know who they are. The Learner has explored the depths of their personality. Maybe they don’t know every aspect of themselves, but they understand the gist. The Learner is able to be themselves in the face of adversarial criticism, and they are able to sleep well at night. They don't care about what others think of them or how they look, and they aren't seeking to impress anyone. The ability to live without lying to oneself where one's internal manifesto and external actions are in harmony with the laws of nature.
8. **Emotional Maturity:** This is the ability to understand that complaining doesn't help, emotional blackmail is counterintuitive, and other forms of immaturity are likely more caustic than healing. It's the knowing that truth will prevail in the end, and by being true to oneself, maturity is an eventuality.
9. **Stoic Calmness:** This is the ability for a person to manage their emotion. It's not that they are emotionally flat, but they are emotionally prepared. Since they understand they cannot control reality, there will be times when events occur that are or are not favorable. There's a deep appreciation for either, and in the face of them, there is a deep feeling, but a calm surface. It's when the captain sails on a glassy, calm water surface. He throws the heavy anchor, but instead of a huge splash, there's hardly any reaction, yet the anchor sinks deep. Thus, any emotionally turbulent event does have a meaningful, even savory, impact for a Learner: it just doesn't show much.

#### Self-reflective
This represents a person's ability to check themselves and assess their life with honesty and humility over hubris and denial. 

**Qualities of being self-reflective:** ^self-reflective
1. **Emotional Responsibility:** This quality is about managing relationships with other people. A person is able to understand when they are not feeling their best emotionally. As a result, they monitor how they treat others. They see it as their task to manage their emotions first. A Learner understands it's OK to seek help when the emotions are too confusing, and they find help through their support system of family, friends, and professionals. Most importantly, a person who is emotionally responsible knows that hiding, suppressing, or not expressing emotions is harmful to the self and others around them. A Learner doesn't hide, but they face emotions not as scary monsters, but educational peers.
2. **The Whole Self:** A Learner doesn't believe in the idea that they are broken somehow, that they must attach themselves to people or things, that some positive or negative force is affecting them, or that someone else completes them as a person. They are a whole person already, and while there are many blessings that come with a complementary companion, they are not an essential part of the self. This balloons into a broader aspect: faith in humanity. Every person is worth their true effort to improve. A Learner sees people as generally good, trustworthy, and that they don't need saving, just a little guidance. No one is broken; everyone is whole, and they're exactly where they need to  be.
3. **Listen to Silence**: This is the ability to sit alone and without distractions, ask yourself leading questions, and to listen to the answers you tell yourself. Every person has the ability to sit in solitude and go inward. This means that you give yourself an interview to learn more about yourself. 
4. **Belief Anchor:** A person's belief system allows or disallows things to affect them. For instance, if a person is afraid of public speaking, it could be for a number of reasons. Most likely is the lack of practice, but some people may believe they can't do it because it's an irrational fear. They allow or give permission to this fear to affect them. They see that something *can* affect them, which is an awareness that would fit in the Witness. They go further to believe that something *is* actively affecting them, thus giving power to belief. It's like a captain who wants to launch his ship and go sailing, but he sees a storm that he believes is coming for him specifically. He chooses to stay on land! A Learner with a belief anchor says, "I see that storm that might be a problem for me, but I choose to sail anyway." It's a way to speak one's power and make it be the winner. Who said perception is reality? Maybe it should be belief begets bounty!
5. **Active Listener:** A person who jumps to conclusions, makes glaring assumptions, and, knowingly or unknowingly, ignores clarification is not listening. They could be confident and well-meaning or purposely harmful. It's as though another captain is sailing their ship, but they are still standing at the helm! They might be filling in the unknown blanks of the shared reality with their insecurities from their personal reality. Whether it's from conditioned behavior, learned helplessness, or unexamined beliefs, their behavior can quickly deteriorate a situation. A Learner approaches every person with ears intent on listening, clarifying when they encounter the slightest confusion.
6. **Mentally Healthy:** This goes hand-in-hand with emotional intelligence. A person cannot expect to function well in the world when they have mental crises caused by avoiding painful emotions. There are real mental health problems that may or may not affect a person's ability to be a Learner. For instance, it may be difficult for a person without brain activity to think about their existence. A Learner regularly takes a self-administered diagnostic checkup on their mental health. They process their emotions appropriately. An optimal mental ability can only help a person find their place in reality. 
7. **Moral Integrity:** The origin of morality is a question that many philosophers debate: do morals come from gods, the self, or culture? Whatever the answer, what is morally good or bad is clearly up to the person. A Learner could say that morally bad would be anything that one, in their healthiest states, wouldn’t want done for/to themselves. It's what moves as many people possible toward the ultimate good of universal love. It's when a captain says, "If I were at my strongest, clearest, and being true to myself, is this the wind that I want filling my sails?" A person must also use their social awareness, objectivity, and systemic understanding to know that everyone is different. Things they wouldn’t mind happening, others might resist, so whatever one decides as morally good or bad should have a diffused edge, not a solid, sudden cut off.
8. **Intuitive:** Everyone has an intuition, which is a gift from one's deepest connection to this reality and all others that (probably) exist. Intuition is the ability to interpret messages from imagination. A Learner understands that imagination is part of intuition; it tells us that other things might be possible in this reality because it thinks outside of it. It dreams up unicorns and dragons, but also unique, practical ideas that may help us in predicaments. First, through imagination, we receive messages that use a personal dictionary of symbols. Next, using emotional feelings, we intuit the meaning of the message. Finally, using reason, we pass the decoded message to our logical mind to figure out the steps of achieving the goal in this reality. Imagination message > Intuition translation > Reason delivery > Logical processing! Intuition is used to guide a person toward their preferences and away from their aversions.

#### Socially-aware
It's important to react to people because we live together. One might be busy, but at least it is fair to respond to someone with kindness when they make an attempt to be social. One may never know if that person they ignored will become important to them later. In a similar sense, it isn't at all fair to release a bad day of emotions on someone else. A person with social-awareness isn't awkward with others or unkind, but they are friendly, kind, and helpful, regardless of their current emotional state.

**Qualities of being socially-aware:** ^socially-aware
1. **Social Tolerance:** The world is filled with people; some of them are likable, and some are not. Regardless, a Learner is able to be near anyone and do so with respect. There isn't a social discrimination or exclusion present in appropriate situations. Everyone has their own way to live, and the Learner is able to live within diversity regardless of their own preference. The world is big enough to share. 
2. **Human, not Robotic:** Be a human in the world, not a robotic appliance. Upon being greeted, a Learner smiles, responds kindly, and shares in the humor of existence. A person nowadays may think it's odd to talk to a passing stranger because of some danger or simply for the number of people around us. The state of a Learner is generally stable enough to allow a fresh perspective where every day is a gift and every person is a reflection of them. Therefore, how can we not connect and notice our similarities first rather than our differences?
3. **Social Balance:** The world is filled with people seeking to press their advantage over others. A Learner helps the innocent when they are weak because of what they feel to be morally right for the sake of one's improvement. They help the guilty when they are overwhelmed by righteousness in excess. For instance, in the face of a bully, a Learner will help the victim. On the other hand, they will protect a known thief against a mob of workers who seek more than a fair punishment. 
4. **Sincerity:** This is not only the belief in and doing the morally right thing and that people who really seek help are worth the trouble. It's also *meaning* it with a full and good heart. People can easily see through faked emotions, and changing oneself to fit situations or other people is a betrayal of the self. People can tell when one is being disingenuous, and it costs nothing to be one's true self. A Learner knows that being sincere is wise because people will give their respect more readily. One should do things because it makes them feel wholesome, not because of how good one looks doing them.
5. **Civic Awareness:** This is the ability to be an active and good citizen in one's city. This means that one behaves appropriately with kind courtesy and polite manners by following rules and laws. One isn't loud, cleans after oneself and pets, doesn’t litter, uses crosswalks and only when it's safe to walk, takes a caring pride in cleanliness, and more. Trashing the space in which one lives is counterintuitive to a healthy lifestyle. It would be as though one's appearance is well groomed, but a pile trash surrounds their house. A Learner understands that respect is not only for people, but the civic and natural environments in which they live.

### The Power of Logic
Although this is our first introduction to structured thinking, it is not the only power in which thinking occurs. In this power, we begin to understand the mind and how it interacts with reality. However, we cannot understand logical thinking without first understanding reasoning. 

Reason is the bridge that connects intuition (which is flexible, unstructured thinking) to logic (sequential, structured). It's how feelings move into contemplation and decision, how a guess becomes a hypothesis. Everyone has intuition, and everyone can reason, but fewer people learn how to use logic.

Logic is the ability to assess reality, reference background knowledge (including instinct, emotions, personal history and education), and think clearly to solve problems reduce risks. Of the three powers we've discussed, logic is the only one we have to learn how to use because logic is a skill. A person doesn't instinctively know how to use logic to its fullest potential because it takes practice.

Logic, in modernity, exists in two different categories: formal and informal logic. Informal logic is the critical-thinking skills of everyday life. Formal logic is the kind of “professional” logic used in academic situations, such as psychology and philosophy. By itself, logic is the type of thinking that deals with facts and truth. 

> [!info|scimitar sheath]
> Intuition is the insight into a higher perspective; logic is the step-by-step application of that insight.
#### Intellectually-humble
This is critical to any person who attempts to understand anything: they know nothing. This comes from the idea that we are all involved in a living experiment, and because of that, the true meaning of the experiment of life and all things in it is unknowable. The experiment involves everything in the universe, even humans, and we don't have the ability to know everything. Knowing something for certain can never (probably) happen, so we live life leaving room for our temporary answers to be updated with more accurate truths.

**Qualities of being intellectually-humble:** ^intellectually-humble

### Power of the Witness

The ship sails where it goes without the captain's will. It might be saddening at first, but it can become similar to the feeling of floating relaxed on a flowing current. As a result, a Learner strives to appreciate all things with a love equal to their own self-love, but the removal of those things only strengthens their importance. One learns to detach oneself from the idea of permanence and embrace the moment, the people, and experiences as fragile temporal treasures. It's like a captain who sails upon a ship that he knows is present now, but will be reduced to splinters by time. He may feel a rueful feeling, but he is more driven by the appreciation he feels for his ship in that moment. A Learner knows that Impermanence gives meaning instead of taking it away.

## About the Cascading Outline
In the program, we use a cascading outline, meaning that what is at the very top of the list is present at every stage below it.  Love occupies this place, and it is the driving force in everything.

Here is a brief description of each category in the outline.

**Powers**
Within love are the powers a person should learn to master: Instinct, Emotion, Logic, the Witness, and the Agent. Each of these are explained below. Further, each power contains a list of personality traits and character qualities.

**Personality Traits**

 - **Instinct:** the self
 - **Emotion:** the others 
 - **Logic:** the solver
 - **Witness:** the observer
 - **Agency:** the action

These are labeled as TX, where T means "trait" and the "X" represents a serial. For example, Instinct is T1, Emotion is T2, and so on.
