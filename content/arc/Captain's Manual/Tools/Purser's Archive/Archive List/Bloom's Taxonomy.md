Developed by Benjamin Bloom in 1956, this uses 6 levels of learning to give structure to professional lessons. 
<br>

Lower-Order Thinking Skills (LOTS):
1. Remember: activate memory or background knowledge  
2. Understand: get meaning from lesson materials by discussion, summary, classification, inference, comparison, demonstration, and explanation.
3. Apply: use a strategy to practice the lesson.

Higher-Order Thinking Skills (HOTS):

4. Analysis: break the material into pieces to see how they work together by organizing, contrasting, and attributing.
5. Evaluate: check any results by critiquing based on certain criteria and learning standards.
6. Create: make something new with the pieces that shows understanding.
</ol>

A lesson doesn't need to use all six levels. For example, a lesson could be an introduction, which may only include level 1 and 2. Below is a verb chart that can help design tasks associated with the levels.
