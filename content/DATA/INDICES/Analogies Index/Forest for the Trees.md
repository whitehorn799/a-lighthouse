# Analogy
Imagine yourself in the middle of a forest. You are intensely focused on one tree, and as you examine the minute details of the bark, the leaves, and the roots, you suddenly feel lost. Nothing has changed for you because you're still focused on the tree. You have no idea where you are, but you know you are standing in front of a tree. You start to see more trees, but they all look the same. You are drawn to the variety of the configurations of branches, the striations of bark, and the transition of green leaves to brown ones. ​

A man approaches you, and he says, "I've never seen you in this forest before. Who are you?"​

You reply, "What forest?"​

The stranger directs you to change your line of sight. He asks you to imagine a higher form of yourself floating in space. That higher self is looking down on your body standing on the surface of the Earth. It sees all perspectives on Earth, and from that vantage, the details of your life seem rather small, even from the perspective of your lifespan as a whole.​

"So you see," he says, "here you are lost in the idea of one singular problem. You don't know you are in a forest because you are looking at only trees. In other words, your problems are rather small in the scope of things. In your whole life and among the problems of the world, they're relatively trivial. Doesn't that make them less dreadful and easier to understand? You can move on, and make better decisions when you see the whole picture because you know more of what's going on. You're no longer lost in detail."


# Interpretation
The trees symbolize the details of a situation, which can be blinding to a person who does not see beyond the details. The forest represents the general situation itself and its impact that the situation has on your life in the long term.

We are often drawn in by our problems, and we get caught in this loop of emotions. We never leave our emotional state to enter into a more logical, problem-solving state of mind. This is to our detriment because we cannot make logical decisions while lost in the details of our problems. We need to change our perspective to one that is much higher. We need to take in more information from many different sources. We need to evaluate how important the problems are by looking at our life as a whole. We start to see that we and our problems are small things indeed.​

By changing your perspective from the "higher self" perspective in outer space, you can see yourself moving through the many details that work together to form our perceived problem. However, from that perspective, the problem becomes less impactful because you see a bigger picture.​

Your higher perspective helps to give you a third-person view of your life; you are on the on the outside of your own life looking in. From there, you gain confidence and agency.
