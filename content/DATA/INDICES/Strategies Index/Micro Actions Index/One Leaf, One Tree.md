>[!invisiblock] 
>As a tree grows, it sprouts leaves that help it grow more, but those leaves may shed after their service ends. For every leaf, the tree goes taller. One leaf contributes to one tree.<br><br>
>You are the tree, and the leaves are the qualities of your [[Self-identity]]. This micro action composes "Identity Notes" that help you build visible momentum toward finding out who you really are. This takes 30 seconds or less.
>
^my-block


>[!invisiblock] 
>Build your beliefs, principles, self-worth, uniqueness, narrative continuity, and agency with this exercise that takes 30 seconds or less.
>1. Choose a time of day for this activity. Try to do it every day at the same time.
>2. Choose a place to do this, like at your desk, on the bus, or on the sofa. Try to do it here every time.
>3. Use sticky notes, a digital widget, or scrap paper to write one of the [[Six-String Guitar|six strings]] of [[Self-identity]]. Write it like this:
>	- "I focus my attention on (1 of the six strings), and today, I choose to (your goal)." 
>	- Here's an example. "I focus my attention on beliefs, and today, I choose to believe that it's okay to ask for help."
>4. It's important to see your progress. You will need a wall for the sticky notes, jars for the scraps of paper, or digital folders for your electronic notes. 
>5. Create three sections using the wall, jars, or folders. Title them "Old Me", "Now Me", and "Goal Me". As you grow, move your notes from one section to the other as you see fit.
>	- For example, suppose you wrote a note about how you react emotionally, and you placed it in the "Now Me" section. If that note no longer applies to you, move it into the "Old Me" section instead of throwing it away. Or, if you accomplish a goal in the "Goal Me" section, move it to the "Now Me".

^my-steps

# How This Helps

1. It helps define who you are and what you prefer. You are indescribable, and sitting down to figure out what you believe is hard. This makes the process easier by focusing one thing at a time. 
2. By choosing a time and a place, it helps build an alarm in your mind that reminds you to do it. When you go to that place and at that time, you will prompt yourself out of habit.
3. It activates your agency by giving you a small, easy task to do. You move from a passive question, "Who am I?" to an active expression of who you are.
4. It helps your emotional brain see that you are consistently choosing agency over being passive. The notes are an undeniable proof of your life's story.
5. It reinforces the idea that claiming your personal power isn't selfish. It's your special contribution to the shared reality, and it requires your presence because it comes from your unique private reality.
6. It shows that knowledge about yourself is an ongoing experiment rather than something that stays the same. You evolve, change, and your experience shapes you. Being able to try on aspects of the six strings for a day helps fine tune your self-worth.
7. You take ownership of the things you write. You acknowledge or dismiss aspects of yourself as you see fit. 
8. It maintains your forward momentum by visually representing all the things that you wrote. The notes are the proof that you matter and that you have agency in the world.
9. You visually see how you change. By using the Old Me, Now Me, and Goal Me sections, you see how you have evolved over time.
10. You honor the Old Me by acknowledging its contribution to your growth. You don't forget your experiences; you cherish them because you see the development they gave you.

### BJ Fogg’s Behavior Model ($B = MAP$)

- **Ability (Simplicity):** Instead of asking them to define their entire identity (high cognitive friction, leads to paralysis), the prompt narrows the focus to **one** small slice of who they are _today_.
    
- **Prompt (Triggers):**
    
    - _Time Anchor:_ First thing upon sitting down at their desk or waking up.
        
    - _Behavioral Anchor:_ Placed on top of their phone or laptop before opening it in the morning.
        
- **Motivation:** Connects the desire for self-worth directly to immediate agency. It shifts them from passively asking "Who am I?" to actively declaring "This is how I express myself today."
    

### Octalysis Framework Alignment

- **Core Drive 1: Epic Meaning & Calling ("We need you"):** Reinforces that claiming their personal power isn't selfish—it’s a contribution to the shared reality that requires their unique presence.
    
- **Core Drive 3: Empowerment of Creativity & Feedback:** Framing self-knowledge as an ongoing experiment rather than a static test. They get to try on different aspects of their identity daily and see immediate real-world results.
    
- **Core Drive 4: Ownership & Possession (Personal Power):** By physically or digitally writing down the statement, they take tangible ownership of an aspect of themselves.
    

### Maintaining Momentum: The "Evidence Jar"

To prevent this from feeling like a abstract journaling exercise and to build compounding momentum:

1. **The Proof Collector:** Every completed sticky note goes into a small visual container (a jar, a folder, or a dedicated note tag).
    
2. **Visual Progress (Core Drive 2: Accomplishment):** Over time, seeing the stack grow serves as undeniable, tangible evidence of their "Narrative Continuity"—proving to their emotional brain that they are consistently choosing agency over passivity.

### Psychological & Framework Mechanics

**1. BJ Fogg’s Behavior Model ($B = MAP$)**

- **Ability:** Extreme simplicity. They don't have to rewrite their identity—they just move a physical or digital card.
    
- **Prompt:** A visual cue (e.g., a 3-column physical index card holder on their desk, an Obsidian kanban board, or sticky notes on a wall) acts as an automatic, friction-free daily prompt.
    

**2. Octalysis Framework Alignment**

- **Core Drive 2: Development & Accomplishment (Visual Progression):** Watching notes physically travel from _Goal Me_ $\rightarrow$ _Now Me_, or from _Now Me_ $\rightarrow$ _Old Me_, provides a clear dopamine loop for personal growth.
    
- **Core Drive 3: Empowerment of Creativity & Feedback:** Identity becomes a living canvas rather than a rigid test. They can experiment with a "Goal Me" card for a week and assess how it feels in reality.
    
- **Core Drive 8: Loss & Avoidance (Reframed Positively):** Moving a lingering bad habit or limiting belief into "Old Me" visually prevents them from sliding back into their old "emotional private reality."
    

### Maintaining Momentum: The "Evolution Audit"

To keep the board active rather than static:

- **Weekly Micro-Review (1 Minute):** Once a week, the client looks at the board and asks one question: _"Is there anything currently in 'Goal Me' that I actually practiced this week?"_ If yes, they drag it to "Now Me" instantly.
    
- **Honoring the "Old Me":** Emphasize to the client that "Old Me" isn't a graveyard of failures—it's proof of adaptability and survival. This reinforces their unique narrative continuity.
