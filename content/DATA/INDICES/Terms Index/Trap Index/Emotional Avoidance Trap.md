>[!invisiblock] 
>The emotional avoidance trap describes a person who is stuck knowing what they **should** do, but give up before even trying. As a result, you seek relief.

^my-block

<br>

>[!invisiblock] 
>![[emotional-avoidance-trap.png|center]]
>

^my-image

<hr>

>[!invisiblock|title] Symptoms
>>You may experience one or more of these. Please remember, these are nonjudgmental assessments.<br><br>
>1. You know what to do, but avoid doing them because you want to distance yourself from stress.
>2. You sometimes feel confused or overwhelmed, but you know they're just excuses. 
>3. You are a perfectionist and a procrastinator who believes that if something can't be finished perfectly, then it isn't worth starting at all.
>4. You have a series of beliefs or mindsets ([[Mental Constructs]]) that hold you back.
>

^my-symptoms

# Plan of Attack

>[!invisiblock|title] Focus on
>1. Accepting that you cannot get the shared reality to match your private reality
>2. Accepting that friction is the result of your private reality not matching the shared reality
>3. Using [[Self-Monitoring]] to recognize when it is happening
>4. Stopping the pattern by using the micro actions below

^focus-on

<br>

>[!invisiblock|title] Micro Actions
>1. **Just Five Minutes:** Promise yourself that you will work on the task for 5 minutes, then quit.
>2. **Trash Draft:** Start with the intent to make the worst possible draft of the solution to your problem.
>3. **Step Backwards:** Think of the end goal, then work backwards through the steps. This makes each step a little easier. Keep going until the first step requires minimal effort.
>4. **Intent Implement:** Choose an action that you do every morning. Then, when you do that action, set your mindset for the day. For example, "When *I put on my shoes*, I will *tell myself that facing my fears is safe and healthy *."
>

^micro-actions

# Commentary
> [!invisiblock] 
>One of the biggest traps of all is encountered in so many situations. Your emotions are great tools, but if they're ignored, they're incredible obstacles. <br><br>
>Suppose you have a deep desire to accomplish a goal. If you are strong in your agency and your identity, you may understand the work and sacrifice that come with accomplishing that desire.<br><br> 
>If you are in a more vulnerable state, your subconscious may be yelling for you to avoid the [[Desire#^desire-pains|desire pains]]. As a result, maybe you decide to go the path of least resistance and conserve your energy. Both are actions that numb can your system of desire totally, especially with repeated suppression. <br><br>
>You tell yourself that the struggle to achieve the desire to achieve the goal isn't worth the effort. Like a baby asking for a toy, your desires keep wanting to be realized. However, like a protective parent, your subconscious mind keeps telling your desires to sit down and be quiet. As a result, you unwittingly convinced yourself you want nothing. 
>
