# 1. Physical
AKA Material Self and World
- The physical body and brain
- Cells, atoms, molecules
<br><br>

>[!invisiblock|title] Definition
>Existence that is within a measurable world of material
^physical-existence-def

**Description** <br>
This existence is everything in the [[Shared Reality]]. It includes your brain matter, your body, chairs, planets, galaxies, etc. It deals with facts, or [[Objectivity]]. You can picture this existence as a security camera that records events without applying emotions or interpretations.

<hr>

# 2. Psychological
AKA Mental Self and World
- The mental mind
- Thoughts, feelings, knowledge
- The human spirit
<br><br>

>[!invisiblock|title] Definition
>Existence that is a mental experience of emotions, thoughts, and knowledge
^psychological-existence-def

**Description** <br>
This includes your background knowledge, experiences, traumas, goals, stories, memories, dreams, etc. It's the personal experience, or [[Subjectivity]]. It's easy to think of this as a person's unique experience. 

When your personal beliefs and expectations don't match with what actually happens in the real world, you experience [[Psychological Friction]].

Importantly, what happens after your current life finishes is part of your spirit. These thoughts occur here, whether they are experienced through religion, spirituality, or otherwise.


**Psychological Existence vs Consciousness**
There is a big difference here. Consciousness is the ability to sense; it's the observer and the space in which observations occur.

Psychological Existence, the mind, is the interpretation of observations. It holds your beliefs, principles, agency, narrative continuity (your story), self-worth, and uniqueness, as well as your thoughts and emotions.

Think of Consciousness as a movie theatre, while Psychological Existence is the movie playing on the screen.

<hr>

# 3. Metaphysical
AKA True Experience
- The harmony of facts and emotions
- The acceptance and comfort of existence after death
<br><br>

>[!invisiblock|title] Definition
>Existence that unites mental clarity with physical wellness
^psychological-existence-def

**Description** <br>
This is the near perfect unification of mind and body. You process the psychological friction of the differences in your private world and the real world. You think beyond your current life and into the afterlife. 

In this course, Metaphysical Existence is the result of facing death as not only an inevitability, but as a mystery that you have already personally addressed. Even though you may not be absolutely sure what happens after you die, you are completely comfortable your beliefs regarding life after death.

This begins to take on a spiritual tone, but the purpose here is not to say that a particular spiritual or religious practice is correct. It's simply the idea that you have come to terms with death, and beyond that, you have a feeling that you exist in a new, nonphysical body when the physical one fails.

Science hasn't proven this yet, but humans don't experience all of reality, either. Keeping this view is a healthy practice in being open-minded to what reality is like. Keep the humility of the knowledge that lies beyond the attempts of our best minds here on Earth to prove life after death.


<hr>

# Conclusion

This is not a black-and-white scale where you are either physical or psychological. Everyone has both parts, and some have a deeper spiritual sense. To what degree they are used or not used is a measure of one's state of existence. The goal here is to be open-minded to being wrong about everything, even what you hold to be true for yourself.

A person who leans into Physical Existence lives in the material world: a hyper-focused CEO, a high-performing pop star, or an ambitious politician. At their best, they are masters of real-world action and results. At their extreme, they can become detached from emotion and driven purely by their own accomplishments.

A person who leans into Psychological Existence lives in the internal world of feeling and meaning: a reflective drifter, a spiritual seeker, or an empathetic caregiver. They seek less meaning behind riches or status and instead try understanding what it means to be alive. However, without remaining grounded to physical reality, they risk becoming dissociated with life itself.


Instead, use the depth of your inner world to guide your actions in the outer world with the added comfort of being confident through death itself.
