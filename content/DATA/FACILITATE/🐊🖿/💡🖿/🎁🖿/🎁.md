# Exploratory Questions

## 001 Love as the Ultimate Environment
Move from the anxious state (ego, indoctrination) "How do I get the world to love me" into a grounded state (intuition, agency) "How do I express myself within the sphere of love".

<hr>

> [!announote|tal] 
> Let’s assume that love is everywhere, like the air, and it’s not an emotion we need to earn. It’s the environment you live in, that you breathe in, and accept as truth. <br><br> 
> How does this change your understanding of loneliness or misalignment with your situation?

- Listen for
	- how they describe alignment: if it's something they need to build or they need to relax into
- Guide toward
	- searching for something rather than acknowledging what you already have

> [!announote|tal] 
> If love accepts you for who you are, even if no one else seems to, how does it change your view on feeling underappreciated?
- Listen for
	- whether they say their circumstances blinded them to love or if love actually disappeared
- Guide toward
	- knowing that something can be there without them being able to sense it

>[!announote|tal]
> What would it feel like to know that no matter what you did, you will never be kicked out of the environment of love?
- Listen for
	- whether they feel safe about the idea of making mistakes, being themselves without fear of judgment
- Guide toward
	- feeling safe and forgiving that we sometimes forget the atmosphere of love is always there, even if we feel alone

## 002 Source of Validation
Move from external locus of control (approval) to internal (self-validation)


<hr>

>[!announote|tal]
> Take a moment to review your life choices. If you had to choose only one, when do feel validated the most: 
> 1. when you form strong connections with what you are doing or with people
> 2. when it comes from possessions, recognition, or reputation?


- Listen for
	- whether the client describes external or internal sources 
- Guide toward
	- feeling internal sources because externals may shift and their core person may not shift with them

>[!announote|tal]
> When you make a decision, do you hope for a feeling of peace inside yourself, or do you wait for the people and culture around you to approve of it?
- Listen for
	- whether the client feels safer with external sources 
- Guide toward
	- the nested environment concept
	- If you make choices to please the culture or you let your environment dictate your choices, what happens to your true identity?

>[!announote|tal]
> Recall the last great thing you did. Did you feel happy because you did a good job, or did you wait for someone to notice it before you could feel happy?
- Listen for
	- lived experiences only; whether they feel heavy emotions under expectations or light emotions from satisfaction
- Guide toward
	- light weight of self-validation rather than the heavy weight of approval
	- Whose voice do you hear in your head when you are making a choice: your own or someone else's?

## 003 Uniqueness

>[!announote|tal]
> For a moment, consider what it would be like if life wasn’t a competition, you didn’t have to be the best, and you chose your own lifestyle regardless of what others think. What value would you see about your life?
- Listen for
	- appeals to external validations
- Guide toward
	- internal validations
		- Take away your job titles and social roles. What is left? What is the special tone you bring to a room just by existing in it?

>[!announote|tal]
> If you were a string on a guitar, what kind of tone do you naturally produce—is it warm, sharp, quiet, or vibrant? How does sharing that tone make the lives of the people around you better or more complete?
- Listen for
	- vanity or modesty in their responses and remind them that love is the environment; everyone is equal
- Guide toward
	- deeper connections to how people's lives would be different without them
		- If you are uncomfortable talking about your uniqueness, aren't you offering silence where there should be a voice? Remember that a song needs low notes as much as it needs high notes
		- Let's think of the opposite way.
			- If the tone is described as harmonious and warm, what about when your tone is altered by your mood or external events?
				- Does your internal structure resist external influences?

>[!announote|tal]
> If we removed you from the world and everyone else suddenly looked, thought, and acted exactly like you, what beautiful thing would the world be missing out on because your special, individual voice was completely gone?
- Listen for
	- an avoidance of simple undeniable truths
- Guide toward
	- magnifying their ability
	- If you weren't here to do that, who would fill that space for your closest friends? That is your uniqueness in action.
		- What is something that you can kind of do well or that no one else can do?
	- In what way does your uniqueness represent a service to the environment of love?
		- How is ignoring or minimizing uniqueness honoring the connection to love?
		- Your thoughts, beliefs, decisions, cell arrangements, experiences, and more have all aligned to create a perspective in a universe that only you can occupy.

## 004 Principles

>[!announote|tal]
> How do you distinguish between a rigid, inherited rule you follow out of habit (indoctrination) and a living, evolving principle that genuinely anchors your internal stability when your external environment changes?
- Listen for
	- vulnerabilities because they are signals of misaligned principles
- Guide toward
	- the ability to test principles against intuition and preferences
	- When you examine a principle of yours very closely, what feeling comes to you: is it one of deep resonance, a shallow indifference, or a sharp dissonance?
		- What are some things that you would defend no matter what anyone says?
		- What are some things that you think are a little unfair and that you would stop if you could?
	- Do the principles you hold align with your internal world because you have selected them personally, or are they a construct formed for you by the external world because you never really thought about them?

>[!announote|tal]
> Think of a time you had to stand your ground against immense pressure from the immediate environment (friends, family, or work) to do something that didn't align with your principles. What was the internal core value that gave you the strength to say "no" and protect your space?
- Listen for
	- any confusion between boundaries and principles
- Guide toward
	- boundaries are how others may treat you; principles are how you treat yourself
	- If you said "no", were you trying to deflect the immediate environment's behavior (boundary), or were you attempting to respect your dignity (principle)?
		- In what way is telling someone how you want to be treated not an attempt to control their behavior?
		- If someone doesn't respect your principles after being informed of them, what would you do as a consequence?
			- In what capacity is your consequence either external (horizontally or emotionally driven) or internal (vertically or transcendentally driven)?
			- Which option is more conducive to the environment of love: explaining why you choose to walk away for the moment or continuously arguing in an emotional state?
	- If you said "yes" every time even when it stood against your principles, how do you compare yourself to anything else that is oppressed or controlled by another? How does that end?

>[!announote|tal]
> What is one rule you have made for yourself that you try never to break, no matter how difficult things get? How does keeping that promise to yourself make you feel strong?
- Listen for
	- how they defend their principles and push to find out if it is too much or too little
- Guide toward
	- realizing how not defending your principles could cause hardships
	- deepening their principles and push to see how far it goes
		- If someone rejects your principles and continues to interact with you regardless of their relationship to you, how far would you let them go and for how long?
		- There are many people in the world, but is it necessary to like and be likable to them all?
		- How do you interpret the saying, "If you stand for nothing, you'll fall for anything"?
		- Do you find standing up for yourself to be a disrespectful act toward others? If so, is it a disrespectful act toward yourself to not defend yourself?

## 005 Beliefs

>[!announote|tal]
> When you look at your core beliefs, how do you distinguish between a conviction you chose through your internal environment (intuition) and a conviction subtly given to you by your external, immediate environment (indoctrination)?
- Listen for
	- defensive rationalizations
- Guide toward
	- questioning the truth of the situation
	- Is your belief an absolute truth of the universe or is it something you adopted to fit into the immediate environment more comfortably?
	- How can you discern between a belief that aligns with you and a belief that misaligns with you?
	- Why do you suppose that someone would want to appropriate your belief, whether through good or bad intentions?
	- How does monitoring your belief system prepare you for interacting with the immediate environment in a way that is more supportive of your internal environment?

>[!announote|tal]
> What is a current belief you hold about yourself that stops you from taking action or claiming your voice? If someone were to completely erase that belief from your mind today, what is the very first thing you would choose to do?
- Listen for
	- limiting beliefs, learned helplessness, or conditioned behaviors
- Guide toward
	- moving away from labels given by the external environment or their own behaviors
	- Who gave you that label: your internal environment (intuition), your external environment (other people), or your own limiting beliefs, learned helplessness, or conditioned behaviors?
	- In what way do our past experiences shape our current beliefs, for better or worse?
		- How can we reverse the effects of our own limiting behaviors and past conditioning?


>[!announote|tal]
> Have you ever let someone make you believe that you couldn't do something, only to find out later that you actually could? How did it feel when you realized they were wrong and you had the power all along?
- Listen for
	- accepting judgments from the external environment as truth
- Guide toward
	- reclaiming the beliefs and power that the external environment stole or molded
	- Why do you suppose that person wanted you to believe you didn't have the ability to do it in the first place?
		- If you believe they wanted to suppress you, would you consider their action to be a conscious and intentional behavior or an unconscious reaction?

## 006 Agency

>[!announote|tal]
> When a sudden crisis or chaotic event disrupts your immediate environment, how do you actively establish a boundary of calm so that your next move is a deliberate, self-authored choice rather than an emotional reaction to the panic around you?
- Listen for
	- whether they think behaving emotionally is conducive to the situation
- Guide toward
	- taking decisive actions for yourself knowing that other people will do the same for them;
	- bringing along loved ones with decisive action who are too emotional to be concerned for personal safety;
	- everyone else are individuals; it's not your job to manage them, especially in crises
	- Running around in emotional panic looks like action, but how does that apply to the solution of the situation?
	- Why would moving with a crowd of people behaving emotionally in a situational crisis or any other event be personally detrimental?

>[!announote|tal]
> Think of a time when everyone around you was rushing to do one thing, but your intuition told you to pause and wait. Did you find the strength to hold your ground and choose your own timing, or did you let the momentum of the crowd pull you along?
- Listen for
	- whether the client thinks that the crowd knows better than they do 
- Guide toward
	- grounding them in the physical sensation of making a choice that matters
	- When you moved against the emotional crowd in favor of your own agency, how did it feel?
		- Where did that strength come from?
		- How did it feel when you realized you didn't have to react the same way everyone else was?
	- Imagine a time when you followed the crowd and something terrible happened. How would you feel if you played a role in hurting someone else through your emotional actions, either influenced by the crowd or not?


>[!announote|tal]
> When you have a really tough, stressful day, do you let the bad day dictate how you treat yourself and others, or can you step back and decide to choose your own attitude anyway?
- Listen for
	- whether the client says that small irritations in people's behavior drives them over a line of tension that's been building up
- Guide toward
	- simplifying the locus of control to intrinsic methods
	- Despite what maladies happen to you throughout your day and the small irritations that set you over the limit, how do you feel after taking out everything on an innocent person that you love?
		- If the guilt from this affects you accordingly, how can you take a step back, look at the behavior and what caused it, and approach that same situation in a different way?
	- Even if the immediate environment gives you a stressful life, how does the idea of your own personal agency (choice) line up with the ultimate environment of love?
	- As a person playing their own role in life, in what way do you believe that your actions actually matter?

	- If you were invalid, bedridden, and couldn't help anyone, would you still be automatically precious to the environment of love?
	- Taking a look at your life now, where do you see that someone or something may be devaluing your own sense of self-worth?
		- How can you end this devaluation? (Using the six strings, particularly: principles)

>[!announote|tal]
> Think about the last time you exhausted yourself trying to make someone else happy or proud of you. What were you afraid would happen to your worth in their eyes if you simply said "no"?
- Listen for
	- reliance on the transactional trap, wherein they gave their energy for approval
- Guide toward
	- spending energy more wisely in a way that is aligned with their internal environment and the environment of love
	- Think of your most important value. Does that comes from the internal or the immediate external environment?
	- What exactly changes for you if you already realize your self-worth and value to others is already defined without having to do anything?
	- After looking at your life now, for whom do you feel you must provide a transactional performance?
		- How can you change this relationship in a way that is both satisfactory for the immediate environment and your intuition?

>[!announote|tal]

Why is it so easy to see value in them, but so hard to see it in yourself when you make a mistake?
- Listen for
	- the lack of self-compassion and forgiveness
- Guide toward
	- self-love and its lessons
	- If everything around you that made you feel valuable and worthy were to instantly change right now, would you feel the same, or would you feel a sense of hopelessness after everything that defined you disappeared?
	- What mistake is hanging over your head that you haven't forgiven? What fear is chasing you instead of walking beside you as a teacher?

# Thought Experiments



## 001 The Ultimate Environment

> [!actbox] 
> We are going to look at the three distinct horizons of your existence right now, from the outside looking in.<br><br>
> First, look at your Immediate Environment. This is the surface script you navigate every day. Look at the country you live in, the culture surrounding you, the traditions of your family, and the demands of your work. Observe the unwritten rules of these systems. Notice the friction—the constant pressure to conform, to produce, and to play the localized human roles assigned to you.<br><br>
> Next, widen your lens to the Macro Environment. Look past the human construct and observe the literal space you occupy. You are a biological organism standing on the crust of a planet spinning through a silent, deep universe. Feel the gravity holding you down. Look at the absolute indifference of the physics engine—the weather, the passage of time, the turning of the seasons, and the vast scale of the cosmos. Within that, you are a human with human nature and spirit.<br><br>
> Now, pull your awareness all the way down into your internal environment. Step into the quiet baseline of your core being. This is the realm of your raw intuition. <br><br>
> Finally, notice the background context that contains all three. The human culture, the physical universe, and your inner being all exist inside a single, silent, all-encompassing reality. If that underlying reality were hostile, existence would collapse. But it is here, holding your breath, holding the stars, and accepting the entire design without judgment.

>[!announote|tal]
> Look at the relationship between your three spheres and love. Are you managing all external things and living in such a way as to fulfill your role for the environment of love.

- Listen for
	- the focus being the immediate environment
- Guide toward
	- pulling back into the internal environment
	- External
		- Are you living your life based on internal clarity, or are you letting the noise of your country, your traditions, or your family drown out your native being?
	- Internal
		- When you drop the cultural scripts of your country and the physical scale of the cosmos, what is the frequency sitting inside you? 
		- Are you in alignment with your own truth, or is your internal space flooded with the noise of the outside world?
		- If the entire system already allows you to exist exactly however you choose to be, would you grant that same total acceptance to your own internal being?

	- If you blend in or stand out, aren't you depriving the environment of love the one thing you were designed to give?
	- If you are fulfilling your role in the ecosystem of love, how does it feel to simply realize all you have to do is to be yourself?

## 004 Speaking Up For Yourself
#PRINCIPLES, #AGENCY 

> [!actbox] 
> Imagine you are sitting at a restaurant, and you look over at a nearby table. The people there are eating a fish dish that looks absolutely incredible—perfectly prepared and beautifully presented.<br><br>
> Inspired by how good it looks, you order the exact same meal. However, when the waiter brings your plate, your fish looks significantly worse—it's sloppy, poorly prepared, and clearly not up to the standard of the one you just saw.<br><br>
> You politely point out the difference to the waiter, but instead of fixing it, he gets defensive, rolls his eyes, and tells you, "That's just how it comes out sometimes. Take it or leave it."


>[!announote|tal] 
> Would you say something to the waiter, or would you eat what is given to you?

- Listen for
	- whether the client shrinks against confrontation, doubts their agency or self-respect, or escalation
- Guide toward
	- realizing their value by standing up for themselves with emotional restraint
	- Staying Silent
		- If you stay silent, are you allowing the immediate environment to tell you your value?
		- If you always accept what is given to you, how can you be sure that you are protected against any discrimination?
		- Why do you sacrifice your self-worth for the sake of perhaps a casual mistake?
		- In a way, are you telling yourself you are inferior to or somehow matter less than other people?
	- Escalation
		- You are spending a lot of energy trying to get the immediate environment to bend to what you want to happen.
		- If you say something for yourself here, does it feel like anger or does it feel a calm sense of confusion?
		- If you become embarrassed or feel the need to retaliate against the waiter or restaurant, does winning a fight make you feel validated?
			- Can you protect your principles without being affected by other people's negative energy?
	- Balanced
		- Use calm, polite behavior to confirm whether a mistake has been made without accusatory language. See the situation as an object you are examining rather than developing an emotional attachment to it.
			- If there is some kind of mistake, what would be more beneficial in the moment: giving someone the benefit of the doubt and seek clarification or remedy? Or is it better to assume the mistake is a personal attack and emotionally retaliating?
			- What is the importance of attempting to convince someone versus the action of continuing to participate in a situation where your principles are violated?
				- If such people who violated your principles come to you later and attempt to work with you again, what attitude would you expect from them and what attitude would you present to them?
			- If the mistake is confirmed, how can calmly stating your action as the consequence to the mistake be demonstrative of the environment of love?
			- What is the use of allowing a negative experience to dictate how you feel for any extension of time?

## 007 Intrinsic Self-worth
#SELF-WORTH #PRINCIPLES


> [!actbox] 
> There was once a beautiful mural in a village next to a deep well. Everyone stopped by the mural each day to admire its beauty. When a drought came, no one came to see the mural, but they all came to draw the bucket from the well.<br><br>
> In a nearby village, there was a tree which grew straight and one which grew twisted. Lumberjacks came and immediately cut down the straight tree, but they left the twisted one alone.<br><br>
> In the valley below, a wildflower bloomed beautifully where no human had ever ventured. A bird landed nearby and asked, "Why do you bloom so beautifully here where no one can see you?" The flower replied, "I don't bloom for them. My beauty is my nature."

>[!announote|tal] 
> Considering the well, the twisted tree, and the wildflower, did their inherent worth change based on how people viewed them, or was it a basic part of who they were?

## 008 The Observing Observer

#NARRATIVE-CONTINUITY #AGENCY #SELF-WORTH


> [!actbox]
> Suppose that you are hired as an actor to play a character in a movie. You understand your role in the story, and through hours of effort, you finally finish the movie. You decide to watch your performance on a big screen in a movie theatre, and you sit down comfortably.<br><br>
> As the movie rolls on, you smile with understanding about how your character's life progresses through the movie. You watch as your character is navigating their deepest struggles, their past heartbreaks, their moments of intense isolation, and their quietest fears. You don't judge their mistakes, you don't scold them for being afraid, and you don't flatter them when they win. You are witnessing your own portrayal of that character's story. As you do so, you notice how their hardships form a kind of patina.<br><br>
> In the movie, the character you portray looks into the camera, and they speak your real name. You know that they are smiling to you. Your character reacts happily to your surprise. They wave at you, and comment on how you were with them their whole movie journey. <br><br>
> By breaking that fourth wall, addressing you, and responding to your reaction, the illusion shatters. You realize that the struggling character on the screen is just a role being played, and you compare that to your own life. You shift your perspective using your imagination. <br><br>
> You zoom out of your body and use your imagination to see yourself from above. You suddenly understand that your life is also a role being played. You see that you are the vast, silent Actor who knowingly, intentionally stepped into this complex script to experience the contrast of this specific movie of your life.

>[!announote|tal] 
> When you look back down at the character's struggles from this high vantage, are those hardships a tragedy that broke you, or are they beautifully orchestrated events you need to experience for progress?

- Listen for
	- avoidance of seeing themselves as a character in a story
- Guide toward
	- breaking the fourth wall of their own life and seeing their lives from a higher perspective
	- If your daily ego is just an actor playing a carefully constructed role, why are we letting the character's temporary script dictate our permanent peace? 
		- What happens to your anxiety when you realize you are the one who is watching the movie, not the one trapped inside the screen?
	- If you, from this higher vantage point, knowingly chose this specific, complex role to experience your own power, what is the current scene trying to teach your character to prepare them for the third act?
	- If a professional actor learns to not hate their characters but play them with absolute respect and devotion, can you grant your own character that same objective reverence? Can you step back that space behind your eyes, hold steady, and watch your story unfold without needing to fight the script?
